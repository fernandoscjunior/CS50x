sort1 uses: Bubble Sort

How do you know? It gets much faster when the numbers are all sorted.
bubble sort's Big O is n^2 but its best case (omega) is just n, so in the best case scenario it just goes one time through the numbers,
and that means it gets faster when already sorted.

sort2 uses: Merge Sort

How do you know?: It was the fastest, as I learned n log n is the fastest.
Merge sort has Big O and Omega of n log n. so in any case it is just very fast.

sort3 uses: Selection Sort

How do you know?: Its performance is consistent through random, reversed and sorted numbers.
Since it has Big O and Omega of n^2, even in the best case when all numbers are sorted it takes the same amount of time to execute then
when it is random.
